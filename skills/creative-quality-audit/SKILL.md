# Creative quality audit

## Inputs and deliverable

- Accept: film or storyboard; creative brief; known constraints.
- Produce: issue register with evidence, severity, cause, fix and recheck.
- If critical inputs are missing, state assumptions, distinguish planning from observed footage, and mark any untestable decision for review. Do not invent measurements, assets or runtime results.

## Decision procedure

1. Review once for intended viewer experience without technical notes, then reconstruct narrative and emotional progression.
2. Review sequences for information timing, performance, composition and lighting; review boundaries for spatial, temporal and state continuity.
3. Identify observed evidence by shot ID/time, distinguish actual defects from taste, deliberate style and unverified assumptions.
4. Rank by effect on comprehension, emotion, continuity and feasibility; fix root causes first rather than accumulating surface tweaks.
5. Propose concrete alternatives and retest the affected sequence; label what cannot be assessed without rendered footage or audio.

## Diagnose and correct

Do not assign a numerical quality score unsupported by evidence. A visually polished shot can fail narrative purpose; a rough intentional shot may succeed.

## Output checks

- Deliver the specified artifact, with decisions tied to identifiable scenes, beats or shots when relevant.
- Show evidence or reasoning for the selected approach and at least one plausible alternative when the decision is consequential; state whether it is planned or actually observed.
- Verify the specific failure mode above is not present, or document the creative reason and the test that would demonstrate success.
- Use the original practice case below to check that the procedure produces a concrete, reviewable result rather than a definition or generic advice.

## Practice case

A sequence has attractive frames but unclear motivation and a reversed eyeline; prioritize story and spatial fixes over adding cinematic effects.
