# Shot flow audit

## Inputs and deliverable

- Accept: ordered shot list/storyboard or footage; intended beats.
- Produce: boundary-by-boundary audit and revised sequence.
- If critical inputs are missing, state assumptions, distinguish planning from observed footage, and mark any untestable decision for review. Do not invent measurements, assets or runtime results.

## Decision procedure

1. For each shot, record the new information, dominant visual element, subject movement and emotional function.
2. For each cut, identify spatial, temporal, logical or graphic relationship and the viewer question answered or created.
3. Check attention transfer, direction, action phase, scale and frame composition; look for repeated emphasis and unintended jumps.
4. Try alternative order, hold, cutaway, shared view or reestablishing shot and compare the intended effect.
5. Separate diagnosed defects from deliberate montage or disorientation; verify the revision in playback or label it as provisional.

## Diagnose and correct

A shot can be correct alone but wrong in context. Do not approve sequence quality using a contact sheet only.

## Output checks

- Deliver the specified artifact, with decisions tied to identifiable scenes, beats or shots when relevant.
- Show evidence or reasoning for the selected approach and at least one plausible alternative when the decision is consequential; state whether it is planned or actually observed.
- Verify the specific failure mode above is not present, or document the creative reason and the test that would demonstrate success.
- Use the original practice case below to check that the procedure produces a concrete, reviewable result rather than a definition or generic advice.

## Practice case

A four-shot scene shows a glance, a distant object, an incompatible reverse and a reaction; diagnose the missing spatial link and repair it.
