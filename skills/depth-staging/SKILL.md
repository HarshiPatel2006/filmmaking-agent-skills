# Depth staging

## Inputs and deliverable

- Accept: set geometry; subjects; viewpoint; narrative need.
- Produce: foreground/midground/background plan, focus and separation strategy.
- If critical inputs are missing, state assumptions, distinguish planning from observed footage, and mark any untestable decision for review. Do not invent measurements, assets or runtime results.

## Decision procedure

1. Place relevant elements at distinct depths, choosing overlap and relative size to make relationships readable.
2. Use an oblique view, visible receding surfaces, foreground framing or lines where they support spatial perception.
3. Select tonal separation between planes; a lighter or darker background can isolate the subject without adding unnecessary rim light.
4. Choose whether multiple planes need sharpness or selective focus; avoid blur that destroys important geography.
5. Review the image as silhouettes and as a moving frame; prevent unwanted mergers when people change positions.

## Diagnose and correct

Depth is not synonymous with maximum bokeh. If a scene depends on the viewer seeing a distant figure, keep enough detail in that plane.

## Output checks

- Deliver the specified artifact, with decisions tied to identifiable scenes, beats or shots when relevant.
- Show evidence or reasoning for the selected approach and at least one plausible alternative when the decision is consequential; state whether it is planned or actually observed.
- Verify the specific failure mode above is not present, or document the creative reason and the test that would demonstrate success.
- Use the original practice case below to check that the procedure produces a concrete, reviewable result rather than a definition or generic advice.

## Practice case

A visitor appears beyond a seated character in a doorway; compose both layers so the reveal remains readable.
