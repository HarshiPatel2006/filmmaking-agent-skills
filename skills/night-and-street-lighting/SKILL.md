# Night and street lighting

## Inputs and deliverable

- Accept: night location; practicals; weather; movement; intended mood.
- Produce: night light map with ambient level, practicals, reflection and exposure checks.
- If critical inputs are missing, state assumptions, distinguish planning from observed footage, and mark any untestable decision for review. Do not invent measurements, assets or runtime results.

## Decision procedure

1. Establish night through relative illumination, isolated sources and sky/environment cues, not simply darkening an image.
2. Choose motivated streetlamps, windows, vehicle light or moon/sky; keep direction and practical spacing coherent in world space.
3. Design subdued ambient bounce to retain required details; balance practical highlights and reflections against subject readability.
4. For moving subjects, plan plausible entry into and exit from light pools; include enough visibility for important gestures.
5. Compare wide and close coverage; if moonlight is stylized, state that choice and maintain it consistently.

## Diagnose and correct

Avoid featureless black or uniformly blue footage and unexplained bright faces in empty darkness. Preserve a believable source relationship.

## Output checks

- Deliver the specified artifact, with decisions tied to identifiable scenes, beats or shots when relevant.
- Show evidence or reasoning for the selected approach and at least one plausible alternative when the decision is consequential; state whether it is planned or actually observed.
- Verify the specific failure mode above is not present, or document the creative reason and the test that would demonstrate success.
- Use the original practice case below to check that the procedure produces a concrete, reviewable result rather than a definition or generic advice.

## Practice case

A traveler walks through a deserted street from one lamp pool to another; describe readable motion and the consistent source geometry.
