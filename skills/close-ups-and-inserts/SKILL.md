# Close ups and inserts

## Inputs and deliverable

- Accept: scene beat; subject; surrounding framing; action continuity.
- Produce: close-up/insert specification with in/out cue, eyeline and narrative purpose.
- If critical inputs are missing, state assumptions, distinguish planning from observed footage, and mark any untestable decision for review. Do not invent measurements, assets or runtime results.

## Decision procedure

1. Identify what the viewer cannot read adequately in the current shot; close in only when additional detail or emphasis matters.
2. Choose cut-in for detail within the established scene or cutaway for a meaningful related action; select reaction timing relative to stimulus.
3. Match subject angle, gaze, lighting, hand position and action phase where continuity is intended.
4. Leave enough composition and context to identify the subject; choose tighter framing or obstruction deliberately.
5. Use multiple sizes sparingly and test the cut in sequence; ensure the close shot changes perception, not just magnification.

## Diagnose and correct

An insert of a prop may mislead if its location or owner is unclear. Establish its relation before cutting tighter or follow with clarifying reaction.

## Output checks

- Deliver the specified artifact, with decisions tied to identifiable scenes, beats or shots when relevant.
- Show evidence or reasoning for the selected approach and at least one plausible alternative when the decision is consequential; state whether it is planned or actually observed.
- Verify the specific failure mode above is not present, or document the creative reason and the test that would demonstrate success.
- Use the original practice case below to check that the procedure produces a concrete, reviewable result rather than a definition or generic advice.

## Practice case

A hand pauses before returning a ring; plan a detail shot and a reaction shot, then decide if both are necessary.
