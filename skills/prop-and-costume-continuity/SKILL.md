# Prop and costume continuity

## Inputs and deliverable

- Accept: story chronology; characters; props; locations.
- Produce: asset ledger with stable IDs, state changes and continuity photos or references.
- If critical inputs are missing, state assumptions, distinguish planning from observed footage, and mark any untestable decision for review. Do not invent measurements, assets or runtime results.

## Decision procedure

1. Choose objects and clothing based on character circumstances, action requirements and visual hierarchy; remove ornamental distractions.
2. Assign stable IDs and specify material, color, scale, wear and ownership; define changes only when justified by story time or action.
3. Map each significant prop and costume state at shot boundaries, including hands, orientation, wetness and damage where relevant.
4. Coordinate values and textures with lighting and exposure; test close views and interactions.
5. Record approved references and compare adjacent coverage; prioritize narratively significant mismatches over tiny invisible differences.

## Diagnose and correct

Avoid arbitrary wardrobe changes within one event or a prop jumping hands between cuts. Correct state rather than merely making a prettier image.

## Output checks

- Deliver the specified artifact, with decisions tied to identifiable scenes, beats or shots when relevant.
- Show evidence or reasoning for the selected approach and at least one plausible alternative when the decision is consequential; state whether it is planned or actually observed.
- Verify the specific failure mode above is not present, or document the creative reason and the test that would demonstrate success.
- Use the original practice case below to check that the procedure produces a concrete, reviewable result rather than a definition or generic advice.

## Practice case

A letter passes between two characters before a time jump; track folds, owner, hand and wardrobe change.
