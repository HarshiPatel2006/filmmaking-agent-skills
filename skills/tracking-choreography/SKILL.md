# Tracking choreography

## Inputs and deliverable

- Accept: story beats; blocking diagram; camera route; lighting plan.
- Produce: choreography timeline with marks, focal targets, transitions and fallback cuts.
- If critical inputs are missing, state assumptions, distinguish planning from observed footage, and mark any untestable decision for review. Do not invent measurements, assets or runtime results.

## Decision procedure

1. Break the take into framing events, each with an intended center of interest and dramatic change.
2. Map actor positions and camera path at each event; test alternative actor-first and camera-first staging.
3. Use overlap, foreground passes or subject movement to motivate transfers of attention without losing geography.
4. Check eyelines, frame edges, focus, light continuity, clearance and pacing at intermediate marks, not just endpoints.
5. Rehearse in rough animatic or video; define cutaway/master alternatives if a single take cannot communicate the event reliably.

## Diagnose and correct

Do not design an elaborate long take only for spectacle. If a major beat is hidden or timing incompatible, divide it into motivated coverage.

## Output checks

- Deliver the specified artifact, with decisions tied to identifiable scenes, beats or shots when relevant.
- Show evidence or reasoning for the selected approach and at least one plausible alternative when the decision is consequential; state whether it is planned or actually observed.
- Verify the specific failure mode above is not present, or document the creative reason and the test that would demonstrate success.
- Use the original practice case below to check that the procedure produces a concrete, reviewable result rather than a definition or generic advice.

## Practice case

A shopkeeper crosses between two customers while passing information; maintain clear emphasis at each of three beats.
