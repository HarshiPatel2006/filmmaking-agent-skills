# Look development

## Inputs and deliverable

- Accept: story tone; frame format; locations; characters.
- Produce: look guide, test matrix, approved visual attributes and exceptions.
- If critical inputs are missing, state assumptions, distinguish planning from observed footage, and mark any untestable decision for review. Do not invent measurements, assets or runtime results.

## Decision procedure

1. Specify visual intention as observable choices: palette, tonal range, light quality, texture, perspective, framing and movement vocabulary.
2. Build a small set of tests that covers recurring environments, faces and action rather than only a hero frame.
3. Compare alternatives against story, continuity and legibility; choose a baseline and justify deviations for specific sequences.
4. Lock shared reference attributes; do not freeze every shot into an identical composition or grade.
5. Check consistency across actual output, not only references; label medium-specific assumptions and avoid copying proprietary imagery.

## Diagnose and correct

A style guide made only of words such as cinematic or premium is not actionable. Describe visible decisions and acceptance examples.

## Output checks

- Deliver the specified artifact, with decisions tied to identifiable scenes, beats or shots when relevant.
- Show evidence or reasoning for the selected approach and at least one plausible alternative when the decision is consequential; state whether it is planned or actually observed.
- Verify the specific failure mode above is not present, or document the creative reason and the test that would demonstrate success.
- Use the original practice case below to check that the procedure produces a concrete, reviewable result rather than a definition or generic advice.

## Practice case

Create look tests for a warm cafe and cool night exterior that share plausible skin and consistent contrast while expressing different emotions.
