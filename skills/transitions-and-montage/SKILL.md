# Transitions and montage

## Inputs and deliverable

- Accept: sequence intent; outgoing/incoming images; time and space relation.
- Produce: transition plan, meaning of juxtaposition, timing and fallback.
- If critical inputs are missing, state assumptions, distinguish planning from observed footage, and mark any untestable decision for review. Do not invent measurements, assets or runtime results.

## Decision procedure

1. State what changes at the boundary: place, time, viewpoint, emotion, idea or action.
2. Prefer a direct cut where it already communicates; choose dissolve, fade, graphic/motion match or other transition only when it expresses a relation or solves a real editorial problem.
3. For montage, order distinct images so each adds information or changes interpretation; compare literal chronology and associative juxtaposition.
4. Check matching graphic shapes, movement, direction and duration; avoid confusing physical continuity with a metaphorical connection.
5. Play transitions in context and remove ornamental effects that distract; reserve stronger devices for meaningful contrast.

## Diagnose and correct

A dissolve does not automatically signal memory, and a matching shape does not guarantee meaning. Establish the narrative relation through context.

## Output checks

- Deliver the specified artifact, with decisions tied to identifiable scenes, beats or shots when relevant.
- Show evidence or reasoning for the selected approach and at least one plausible alternative when the decision is consequential; state whether it is planned or actually observed.
- Verify the specific failure mode above is not present, or document the creative reason and the test that would demonstrate success.
- Use the original practice case below to check that the procedure produces a concrete, reviewable result rather than a definition or generic advice.

## Practice case

Match an empty chair in a present scene with an occupied chair in memory; verify the time distinction and emotional association.
