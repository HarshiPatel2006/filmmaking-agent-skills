# Cutting on action

## Inputs and deliverable

- Accept: adjacent coverage; movement phase; target meaning.
- Produce: edit in/out points or planned cut cues with match checks.
- If critical inputs are missing, state assumptions, distinguish planning from observed footage, and mark any untestable decision for review. Do not invent measurements, assets or runtime results.

## Decision procedure

1. Identify the action phase that carries attention and choose an incoming angle that continues or deliberately reframes it.
2. Compare action, screen direction, subject scale, speed and prop/limb position at candidate cut points.
3. Test cuts slightly before, during and after the action; choose the one that communicates the event and feels continuous at playback speed.
4. If coverage does not match, use an alternate phase, a meaningful reaction or a clear ellipsis rather than forcing a jump.
5. Check both picture and sound continuity; document an intentional abrupt cut as a creative effect.

## Diagnose and correct

Cutting on action is not mandatory for every scene; stillness and delayed reaction can be stronger. Do not repeat the same gesture unintentionally.

## Output checks

- Deliver the specified artifact, with decisions tied to identifiable scenes, beats or shots when relevant.
- Show evidence or reasoning for the selected approach and at least one plausible alternative when the decision is consequential; state whether it is planned or actually observed.
- Verify the specific failure mode above is not present, or document the creative reason and the test that would demonstrate success.
- Use the original practice case below to check that the procedure produces a concrete, reviewable result rather than a definition or generic advice.

## Practice case

An actor opens a drawer across wide and close coverage; align the drawer travel and hand position, then compare three cut points.
