# Exterior lighting

## Inputs and deliverable

- Accept: location; sun/sky conditions; schedule; shot goals.
- Produce: exterior plan with orientation, source control, matching windows and contingencies.
- If critical inputs are missing, state assumptions, distinguish planning from observed footage, and mark any untestable decision for review. Do not invent measurements, assets or runtime results.

## Decision procedure

1. Determine direction and character of available light; scout intended camera orientations and subject movement.
2. Choose time and framing for the desired mood, contrast and background; decide whether to use shade, bounce, diffusion or fill.
3. Avoid destroying natural depth with unnecessary frontal illumination; test faces against bright backgrounds and preserve essential highlight detail.
4. Group matching setups by compatible sun and cloud state; record continuity conditions and weather changes.
5. Design a weather contingency and ensure safety for real locations and crew; treat older film-stock/filter prescriptions as historical context.

## Diagnose and correct

A sunny master and overcast reverse may not cut together. Reorder coverage, adjust lighting or motivate the environmental change.

## Output checks

- Deliver the specified artifact, with decisions tied to identifiable scenes, beats or shots when relevant.
- Show evidence or reasoning for the selected approach and at least one plausible alternative when the decision is consequential; state whether it is planned or actually observed.
- Verify the specific failure mode above is not present, or document the creative reason and the test that would demonstrate success.
- Use the original practice case below to check that the procedure produces a concrete, reviewable result rather than a definition or generic advice.

## Practice case

A conversation at an open field near sunset; plan orientation and timing so shadows stay believable while the story advances.
