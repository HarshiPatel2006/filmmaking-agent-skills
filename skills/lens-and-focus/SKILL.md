# Lens and focus

## Inputs and deliverable

- Accept: subject scale; camera position; sensor/format or virtual camera; motion.
- Produce: perspective and focus plan with assumptions and alternatives.
- If critical inputs are missing, state assumptions, distinguish planning from observed footage, and mark any untestable decision for review. Do not invent measurements, assets or runtime results.

## Decision procedure

1. Set camera distance for perspective: nearer emphasizes depth relationships; farther reduces relative size differences.
2. Choose focal length or field of view for the required framing on the actual format; state equivalents only when the format is known.
3. Choose depth of field for information hierarchy; retain multiple readable planes when interaction requires them.
4. Plan focus target and changes during movement; use a rack focus only when attention should transfer.
5. Test faces, edges, background and focus at start and end; distinguish optical behavior from digital crops or simulated blur.

## Diagnose and correct

Do not prescribe a universal focal length or claim a lens alone changes perspective. Reposition camera or change composition to solve the actual problem.

## Output checks

- Deliver the specified artifact, with decisions tied to identifiable scenes, beats or shots when relevant.
- Show evidence or reasoning for the selected approach and at least one plausible alternative when the decision is consequential; state whether it is planned or actually observed.
- Verify the specific failure mode above is not present, or document the creative reason and the test that would demonstrate success.
- Use the original practice case below to check that the procedure produces a concrete, reviewable result rather than a definition or generic advice.

## Practice case

Two actors stand at different depths; choose between both readable and a focus transfer that reveals one actor's reaction.
