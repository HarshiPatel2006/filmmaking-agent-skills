# Visual information design

## Inputs and deliverable

- Accept: story chronology; viewer knowledge; intended emotion.
- Produce: information map with reveal, withheld fact, cue and payoff.
- If critical inputs are missing, state assumptions, distinguish planning from observed footage, and mark any untestable decision for review. Do not invent measurements, assets or runtime results.

## Decision procedure

1. Record separately what each character knows and what the audience knows at every major beat.
2. Form a question that the audience can recognize through a look, object, action or consequence; do not withhold basic orientation by accident.
3. Choose the reveal order: cause before effect for anticipation, effect before cause for curiosity, or parallel knowledge for suspense.
4. Assign shot-level cues and reactions; decide if an insert, point of view or delayed wide shot reveals the information most effectively.
5. Review the sequence without explanation and verify the intended question is answered or deliberately left open.

## Diagnose and correct

If a twist depends on information the film never made available, introduce a subtle fair cue. If mystery becomes confusion, restore context without overexplaining.

## Output checks

- Deliver the specified artifact, with decisions tied to identifiable scenes, beats or shots when relevant.
- Show evidence or reasoning for the selected approach and at least one plausible alternative when the decision is consequential; state whether it is planned or actually observed.
- Verify the specific failure mode above is not present, or document the creative reason and the test that would demonstrate success.
- Use the original practice case below to check that the procedure produces a concrete, reviewable result rather than a definition or generic advice.

## Practice case

A woman sees an unfamiliar coat before its owner is shown; create both a suspense version and a curiosity version with distinct knowledge timing.
