# Genre and tone

## Inputs and deliverable

- Accept: concept; intended audience experience; reference descriptions.
- Produce: genre promise, tonal boundaries, sequence contrast plan.
- If critical inputs are missing, state assumptions, distinguish planning from observed footage, and mark any untestable decision for review. Do not invent measurements, assets or runtime results.

## Decision procedure

1. Identify the primary emotion promised by the genre; distinguish audience expectation from formula.
2. List the few recurring situations or experiences that deliver that promise and the deliberate departures.
3. Choose a tonal range in performance, composition, light, pace and sound; describe contrasting moments as transitions rather than arbitrary mood shifts.
4. Test the first, midpoint and final sequences for consistency of audience promise.
5. Preserve genre flexibility; do not use historical reel lengths as a modern universal rule.

## Diagnose and correct

If a romance abruptly becomes slapstick with no preparation, specify the narrative motivation and revise adjoining beats or remove the contrast.

## Output checks

- Deliver the specified artifact, with decisions tied to identifiable scenes, beats or shots when relevant.
- Show evidence or reasoning for the selected approach and at least one plausible alternative when the decision is consequential; state whether it is planned or actually observed.
- Verify the specific failure mode above is not present, or document the creative reason and the test that would demonstrate success.
- Use the original practice case below to check that the procedure produces a concrete, reviewable result rather than a definition or generic advice.

## Practice case

A warm family drama contains an unsettling discovery; maintain underlying emotional viewpoint while changing tension and light.
