# Directorial interpretation

## Inputs and deliverable

- Accept: script; intended audience experience; resources.
- Produce: director's scene brief, visual strategy alternatives, decision rationale.
- If critical inputs are missing, state assumptions, distinguish planning from observed footage, and mark any untestable decision for review. Do not invent measurements, assets or runtime results.

## Decision procedure

1. Identify the viewer's necessary knowledge, emotional proximity and the dramatic change in the scene.
2. Describe the visual approach in terms of viewpoint, shot size, composition, movement and tonal pattern rather than generic style adjectives.
3. Generate two or three contrasting staging and coverage strategies; compare information timing and human relationships.
4. Coordinate with design, lighting and editor; decide which moments need coverage and which should play in one sustained view.
5. Test alternatives with a storyboard or rough blocking rehearsal and revise based on shot flow.

## Diagnose and correct

A list of cinematic angles without a scene purpose is not direction. Remove shots that neither convey information nor alter emotional perspective.

## Output checks

- Deliver the specified artifact, with decisions tied to identifiable scenes, beats or shots when relevant.
- Show evidence or reasoning for the selected approach and at least one plausible alternative when the decision is consequential; state whether it is planned or actually observed.
- Verify the specific failure mode above is not present, or document the creative reason and the test that would demonstrate success.
- Use the original practice case below to check that the procedure produces a concrete, reviewable result rather than a definition or generic advice.

## Practice case

A quiet kitchen argument can play in a fixed two-shot or divided close-ups; select based on when the relationship fractures.
