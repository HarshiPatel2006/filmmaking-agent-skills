# Portrait lighting

## Inputs and deliverable

- Accept: subject appearance; scene light map; camera position; dramatic intent.
- Produce: portrait lighting plan with source position, fill, eye and background decisions.
- If critical inputs are missing, state assumptions, distinguish planning from observed footage, and mark any untestable decision for review. Do not invent measurements, assets or runtime results.

## Decision procedure

1. Evaluate face shape and movement under candidate light directions; choose modeling that fits the individual and scene rather than a universal beauty setup.
2. Place the dominant light relative to camera and subject, checking brow, nose and chin shadows; adjust fill to preserve required information.
3. Consider a subtle eye reflection, hair edge or background separation only when motivated; avoid flattening or excessive retouching.
4. Keep apparent direction and contrast compatible with the wider setup; cheat actual placement only as needed for continuity and performance.
5. Compare neutral and emotional expressions, multiple head orientations and recorded highlight/shadow detail; preserve identity and natural texture.

## Diagnose and correct

Do not apply historical gendered beautification assumptions or equate attractiveness with lighter skin. Modify lighting to serve a specific subject and narrative.

## Output checks

- Deliver the specified artifact, with decisions tied to identifiable scenes, beats or shots when relevant.
- Show evidence or reasoning for the selected approach and at least one plausible alternative when the decision is consequential; state whether it is planned or actually observed.
- Verify the specific failure mode above is not present, or document the creative reason and the test that would demonstrate success.
- Use the original practice case below to check that the procedure produces a concrete, reviewable result rather than a definition or generic advice.

## Practice case

A restrained close-up of a tired musician needs visible eyes without losing side-lit texture; compare two source positions and retain the more believable one.
