# Open closed framing

## Inputs and deliverable

- Accept: scene intention; frame format; subject position; offscreen action.
- Produce: framing alternatives and viewer-involvement rationale.
- If critical inputs are missing, state assumptions, distinguish planning from observed footage, and mark any untestable decision for review. Do not invent measurements, assets or runtime results.

## Decision procedure

1. Decide whether the scene should feel like a complete arranged space or a fragment of a larger world.
2. Use offscreen gaze, incomplete objects, entries and exits to imply space beyond the frame when useful.
3. Use enclosing foregrounds, architectural geometry, symmetry or a contained composition for deliberate separation or concentration.
4. Place looking room and movement space based on likely action; purposeful compression may create pressure but must not hide crucial information.
5. Test different crops and neighboring shots; do not treat centered subjects or negative space as inherently correct or incorrect.

## Diagnose and correct

A frame with unexplained cropped action may look accidental. Establish offscreen context or restore the necessary content.

## Output checks

- Deliver the specified artifact, with decisions tied to identifiable scenes, beats or shots when relevant.
- Show evidence or reasoning for the selected approach and at least one plausible alternative when the decision is consequential; state whether it is planned or actually observed.
- Verify the specific failure mode above is not present, or document the creative reason and the test that would demonstrate success.
- Use the original practice case below to check that the procedure produces a concrete, reviewable result rather than a definition or generic advice.

## Practice case

A person waits beside an unseen doorway; compare an open frame implying arrival with an enclosed frame emphasizing isolation.
