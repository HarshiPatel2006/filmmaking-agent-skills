# Subject separation

## Inputs and deliverable

- Accept: subject; background; wardrobe; composition; mood.
- Produce: separation strategy and edge/readability checks.
- If critical inputs are missing, state assumptions, distinguish planning from observed footage, and mark any untestable decision for review. Do not invent measurements, assets or runtime results.

## Decision procedure

1. Compare subject silhouette with background values; identify mergers where they impair the intended hierarchy.
2. Choose lighter background behind dark subject, darker background behind light subject, selective background accents or a justified edge light.
3. Consider changing camera or subject position before adding light; use focus separation only if spatial information remains clear.
4. Balance foreground/background exposure and color contrast; check reflective surfaces and transitions between shots.
5. Review at thumbnail size and normal display size, including moving poses.

## Diagnose and correct

Avoid automatic rim lights on every subject. An obvious halo or flat overlit scene may be worse than a deliberate silhouette.

## Output checks

- Deliver the specified artifact, with decisions tied to identifiable scenes, beats or shots when relevant.
- Show evidence or reasoning for the selected approach and at least one plausible alternative when the decision is consequential; state whether it is planned or actually observed.
- Verify the specific failure mode above is not present, or document the creative reason and the test that would demonstrate success.
- Use the original practice case below to check that the procedure produces a concrete, reviewable result rather than a definition or generic advice.

## Practice case

A dark coat blends into a dark wall; use a motivated wall pool or reposition subject rather than a bright unexplained outline.
