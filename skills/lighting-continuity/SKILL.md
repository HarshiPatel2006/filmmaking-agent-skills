# Lighting continuity

## Inputs and deliverable

- Accept: scene lighting plan; shots; source positions; time continuity.
- Produce: lighting state ledger with permitted adjustments and mismatch corrections.
- If critical inputs are missing, state assumptions, distinguish planning from observed footage, and mark any untestable decision for review. Do not invent measurements, assets or runtime results.

## Decision procedure

1. Record source direction in world coordinates, quality, approximate color, exposure relationship and background pools for each setup.
2. For close-ups, adjust placement to shape faces while keeping apparent direction, shadows and contrast compatible with the established world.
3. For moving shots, map light zones along the path and test transitions; avoid unexplained exposure or shadow changes.
4. Track time of day, clouds, practical on/off state and reflections; schedule matching coverage or document intended change.
5. Compare adjacent frames and representative motion, not isolated beauty frames; fix upstream setup or grade only within recoverable limits.

## Diagnose and correct

Do not require identical pixels across camera angles; preserve physical logic and narrative continuity. Do not conceal severe mismatches with an unrelated color preset.

## Output checks

- Deliver the specified artifact, with decisions tied to identifiable scenes, beats or shots when relevant.
- Show evidence or reasoning for the selected approach and at least one plausible alternative when the decision is consequential; state whether it is planned or actually observed.
- Verify the specific failure mode above is not present, or document the creative reason and the test that would demonstrate success.
- Use the original practice case below to check that the procedure produces a concrete, reviewable result rather than a definition or generic advice.

## Practice case

A reverse angle makes a lamp appear to light the wrong side of a face; reconstruct world source positions and correct the reverse setup.
