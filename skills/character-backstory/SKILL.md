# Character backstory

## Inputs and deliverable

- Accept: character role; present conflict; known story facts.
- Produce: backstory and relationship ledger with disclosure plan.
- If critical inputs are missing, state assumptions, distinguish planning from observed footage, and mark any untestable decision for review. Do not invent measurements, assets or runtime results.

## Decision procedure

1. Work backward from a present behavior to a plausible history; generate alternatives and select the one that supports the current conflict.
2. Identify formative relationships, responsibilities and unresolved events; describe their consequences in present behavior.
3. Assign each relationship asymmetrical needs and knowledge; specify what one person owes, hides or expects.
4. Keep private character notes separate from what the audience must learn; disclose history only when it changes a beat.
5. Check dates, ages, social context and contradictions; revise stereotypes into individual circumstances.

## Diagnose and correct

Avoid adding a traumatic origin merely to justify every trait. Ordinary learned habits and obligations can be more specific.

## Output checks

- Deliver the specified artifact, with decisions tied to identifiable scenes, beats or shots when relevant.
- Show evidence or reasoning for the selected approach and at least one plausible alternative when the decision is consequential; state whether it is planned or actually observed.
- Verify the specific failure mode above is not present, or document the creative reason and the test that would demonstrate success.
- Use the original practice case below to check that the procedure produces a concrete, reviewable result rather than a definition or generic advice.

## Practice case

A bus driver always checks the rear mirror twice before departure; connect the habit to relevant history without overloading the scene.
