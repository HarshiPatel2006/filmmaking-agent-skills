# Exposition and backstory

## Inputs and deliverable

- Accept: world facts; chronology; scene outline; audience knowledge.
- Produce: exposition ledger, release timing, visual/dialogue alternatives.
- If critical inputs are missing, state assumptions, distinguish planning from observed footage, and mark any untestable decision for review. Do not invent measurements, assets or runtime results.

## Decision procedure

1. List the minimum facts a viewer needs to understand the next decision, separating essential facts from optional lore.
2. Assign each essential fact a delivery moment just before it becomes consequential; prefer behavior, environment or disagreement that carries drama.
3. Use backstory to motivate a present choice rather than to explain every trait; distinguish implied history from facts that must be spoken.
4. Where a flashback is used, cue its relationship to present time and ensure it changes interpretation.
5. Remove explanations viewers have already inferred; test the scene order with a reader unfamiliar with the story.

## Diagnose and correct

A long speech about an old friendship can stall action. Give one revealing object or habitual gesture, then disclose the necessary fact only when it changes the decision.

## Output checks

- Deliver the specified artifact, with decisions tied to identifiable scenes, beats or shots when relevant.
- Show evidence or reasoning for the selected approach and at least one plausible alternative when the decision is consequential; state whether it is planned or actually observed.
- Verify the specific failure mode above is not present, or document the creative reason and the test that would demonstrate success.
- Use the original practice case below to check that the procedure produces a concrete, reviewable result rather than a definition or generic advice.

## Practice case

A retired musician avoids a concert hall; establish the relevant past through a present obstacle before disclosing the reason.
