# Weather and atmosphere

## Inputs and deliverable

- Accept: weather condition; scene visibility; source directions; movement.
- Produce: weather specification with density, visibility, light and surface continuity.
- If critical inputs are missing, state assumptions, distinguish planning from observed footage, and mark any untestable decision for review. Do not invent measurements, assets or runtime results.

## Decision procedure

1. Choose the weather's narrative purpose and continuity state; define wind, intensity, direction, wetness and visibility range.
2. For rain, use edge or back illumination against a contrasting background when needed so droplets register; avoid overlit curtains that hide faces.
3. For fog, use distance-dependent contrast and motivated beams; ensure nearby features remain more readable than distant ones unless stylization dictates otherwise.
4. For snow or reflective surfaces, protect highlight texture and manage bounce; test exposure for the actual camera or generated frame.
5. Match environmental conditions, reflections, clothing wetness and motion across shots; use safe simulated alternatives rather than historical hazardous methods.

## Diagnose and correct

Do not paint fog uniformly across every depth plane or switch rain direction between reverse shots. Restore physical consistency.

## Output checks

- Deliver the specified artifact, with decisions tied to identifiable scenes, beats or shots when relevant.
- Show evidence or reasoning for the selected approach and at least one plausible alternative when the decision is consequential; state whether it is planned or actually observed.
- Verify the specific failure mode above is not present, or document the creative reason and the test that would demonstrate success.
- Use the original practice case below to check that the procedure produces a concrete, reviewable result rather than a definition or generic advice.

## Practice case

A couple stands at a misty waterfront after rain; separate foreground expressions from receding background and maintain wet surfaces in coverage.
