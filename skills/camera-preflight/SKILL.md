# Camera preflight

## Inputs and deliverable

- Accept: capture method; location; conditions; output requirements.
- Produce: preflight checklist, test recording notes and fallback.
- If critical inputs are missing, state assumptions, distinguish planning from observed footage, and mark any untestable decision for review. Do not invent measurements, assets or runtime results.

## Decision procedure

1. Verify intended framing, recording mode, available storage/power, media integrity and audio path; test rather than assume device capabilities.
2. Check exposure and highlight/shadow detail, white balance and focus under the actual light; disable or control automation when it causes visible instability.
3. Choose support and movement method appropriate to the shot; test the complete intended move rather than only static framing.
4. Record and review a short sample for focus hunting, shake, clipping, flicker, reflections, audio and sync.
5. Document settings and reset between setups; verify a safe backup and preserve originals.

## Diagnose and correct

Do not reuse obsolete tape or camera-format advice as current requirements. Use measured device behavior and current documentation for technical specifics.

## Output checks

- Deliver the specified artifact, with decisions tied to identifiable scenes, beats or shots when relevant.
- Show evidence or reasoning for the selected approach and at least one plausible alternative when the decision is consequential; state whether it is planned or actually observed.
- Verify the specific failure mode above is not present, or document the creative reason and the test that would demonstrate success.
- Use the original practice case below to check that the procedure produces a concrete, reviewable result rather than a definition or generic advice.

## Practice case

A backlit interview with a moving subject requires a short test showing face exposure, stable focus, clean audio and sufficient storage.
