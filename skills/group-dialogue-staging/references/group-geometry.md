# Staging alternatives

Katz compares arrangements of two, three and larger groups (KT 186–233). Use his geometric approach as a method of generating alternatives, not as a fixed actor choreography.

- Linear arrangement: useful when participants face a shared target or line up; the outer participants may occlude one another or become visually isolated. Try a camera at an oblique angle.
- Angled arrangement: two participants on one side of an exchange and one on another can clarify alliances; a shared composition can establish them before individual coverage.
- Depth arrangement: an observer in the foreground or background can remain visible while others exchange dialogue. Consider focus and tonal hierarchy before separating the observer into a cutaway.
- Mobile regrouping: an actor changes physical position or turns attention to a different person; use the movement to establish a new relationship and action axis.

Practice: two roommates confront a third about an unpaid bill. Compare a shared table composition, an arrangement that isolates the third person, and a moving regrouping where one roommate comes to the third person's side. Choose by the intended relationship change; specify what must be visible on the listener during the exchange. Do not cut to every speaker automatically.
