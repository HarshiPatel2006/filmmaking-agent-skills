# Group dialogue staging

## Inputs and deliverable

- Accept: participants; scene beats; set plan.
- Produce: grouping map, coverage of focal exchanges, reorientation shots.
- If critical inputs are missing, state assumptions, distinguish planning from observed footage, and mark any untestable decision for review. Do not invent measurements, assets or runtime results.

## Decision procedure

1. Start from the social grouping, using line, triangular or angled arrangements where natural; avoid equally spaced heads by default.
2. For every exchange, identify current speaker, listener and the third party whose reaction may matter.
3. Divide large groups into meaningful subgroups; establish each group's relationship with a master or linking composition.
4. When conversation shifts to another pair, establish the new gaze and axis before cutting into singles; retain overlapping visual space where possible.
5. Track changes of focal interest through blocking, focus, lighting or framing; record useful reaction coverage.

## Diagnose and correct

A succession of tight singles loses the room's geography and alliances. Restore the relevant subgroup and their spatial relation.

## Output checks

- Deliver the specified artifact, with decisions tied to identifiable scenes, beats or shots when relevant.
- Show evidence or reasoning for the selected approach and at least one plausible alternative when the decision is consequential; state whether it is planned or actually observed.
- Verify the specific failure mode above is not present, or document the creative reason and the test that would demonstrate success.
- Use the original practice case below to check that the procedure produces a concrete, reviewable result rather than a definition or generic advice.

## Practice case

Three friends argue while a fourth listens at a doorway; map alliances and provide coverage when attention turns to the listener.
