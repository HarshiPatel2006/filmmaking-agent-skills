# Two person dialogue

## Inputs and deliverable

- Accept: scene beats; actor positions; intended intimacy; lens/framing constraints.
- Produce: two-person coverage plan, axis diagram, beat-to-shot map.
- If critical inputs are missing, state assumptions, distinguish planning from observed footage, and mark any untestable decision for review. Do not invent measurements, assets or runtime results.

## Decision procedure

1. Choose a two-shot that establishes relative positions and emotional distance; test angled, profile and shared-depth arrangements.
2. Map a line between participants and cameras on one side; preserve opposing eyelines in singles.
3. Select over-the-shoulder or clean singles according to the desired involvement, retaining the partner's presence when meaningful.
4. Use size and angle asymmetry deliberately when perspective or power changes; match direction and look space rather than forcing mirror images.
5. Capture the listener's important reactions and overlapping action; return to a master or reframe when blocking changes.

## Diagnose and correct

If alternation of identical singles feels mechanical, hold a two-shot over meaningful interaction or delay the reaction cut. Preserve geography.

## Output checks

- Deliver the specified artifact, with decisions tied to identifiable scenes, beats or shots when relevant.
- Show evidence or reasoning for the selected approach and at least one plausible alternative when the decision is consequential; state whether it is planned or actually observed.
- Verify the specific failure mode above is not present, or document the creative reason and the test that would demonstrate success.
- Use the original practice case below to check that the procedure produces a concrete, reviewable result rather than a definition or generic advice.

## Practice case

A cordial interview becomes an accusation; include a shared view, a justified closer view and a clear reason for each cut.
