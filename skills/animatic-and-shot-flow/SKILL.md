# Animatic and shot flow

## Inputs and deliverable

- Accept: storyboard; available audio or scene timing; target runtime.
- Produce: timed panel sequence, provisional edit decision list and issue log.
- If critical inputs are missing, state assumptions, distinguish planning from observed footage, and mark any untestable decision for review. Do not invent measurements, assets or runtime results.

## Decision procedure

1. Arrange panels and temporary audio in a coherent order; use known audio events or action beats as anchors, not arbitrary equal intervals.
2. Assign provisional durations based on information density, movement and reaction needs; test alternate timing at playback speed.
3. Flag missing spatial links, unclear looks, repetitive shot scales and dramatic turns without coverage.
4. Refine the board or request a missing panel before polishing assets; maintain versioned shot IDs.
5. Treat the animatic as a timing and intent test, not proof that final movement or performance will work.

## Diagnose and correct

Static panels can conceal impossible motion. Add rough movement, diagrams or text at transitions where needed.

## Output checks

- Deliver the specified artifact, with decisions tied to identifiable scenes, beats or shots when relevant.
- Show evidence or reasoning for the selected approach and at least one plausible alternative when the decision is consequential; state whether it is planned or actually observed.
- Verify the specific failure mode above is not present, or document the creative reason and the test that would demonstrate success.
- Use the original practice case below to check that the procedure produces a concrete, reviewable result rather than a definition or generic advice.

## Practice case

A 30-second sequence feels rushed at its reveal and slow at routine travel; revise timing and document the intended edit points.
