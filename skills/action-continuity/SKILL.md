# Action continuity

## Inputs and deliverable

- Accept: shot list; action beats; footage or planned states.
- Produce: state ledger and matching-action edit map.
- If critical inputs are missing, state assumptions, distinguish planning from observed footage, and mark any untestable decision for review. Do not invent measurements, assets or runtime results.

## Decision procedure

1. Record starting and ending poses, hand/prop states, screen positions, gaze, motion phase and relevant dialogue timing.
2. For overlapping coverage, repeat enough action on both angles to provide a choice of cut point; track the exact phase rather than only start time.
3. Match action phase, direction, speed and emotional intensity across a cut where continuous time is intended.
4. If continuity is impossible, redesign cut timing, use a genuinely related reaction or establish an intentional time gap; do not use a cutaway to conceal a changed fact that matters.
5. Inspect frames around edit points and play at normal speed; flag unexplained changes and distinguish minor invisible cheats from story errors.

## Diagnose and correct

A glass changes hands or a door closes twice across a cut. Choose matching frames or revise the sequence.

## Output checks

- Deliver the specified artifact, with decisions tied to identifiable scenes, beats or shots when relevant.
- Show evidence or reasoning for the selected approach and at least one plausible alternative when the decision is consequential; state whether it is planned or actually observed.
- Verify the specific failure mode above is not present, or document the creative reason and the test that would demonstrate success.
- Use the original practice case below to check that the procedure produces a concrete, reviewable result rather than a definition or generic advice.

## Practice case

A character lifts a cup in a wide shot and drinks in a close-up; check hand, cup position and motion phase at the cut.
