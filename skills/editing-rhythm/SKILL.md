# Editing rhythm

## Inputs and deliverable

- Accept: shot sequence; dialogue/music timing; emotional objective; target runtime.
- Produce: timed edit map with motivated cut points and pacing notes.
- If critical inputs are missing, state assumptions, distinguish planning from observed footage, and mark any untestable decision for review. Do not invent measurements, assets or runtime results.

## Decision procedure

1. Identify major dramatic and musical phrases or action events; distinguish observed timing from provisional planning.
2. Estimate each shot's required reading time and performance duration; cut when the next image adds information, changes emphasis or advances rhythm.
3. Use shorter or longer holds as expressive contrast; avoid fixed-length blocks or mandatory cuts on every musical beat.
4. Compare several edits at playback speed; preserve the soundtrack's continuity when it is the timing authority.
5. Record timebase, in/out points, reasons and alternatives; check total duration and frame-compatible boundaries when technical inputs are known.

## Diagnose and correct

A beat grid does not replace editorial judgment. If equal shots feel mechanical, vary them around actual phrase boundaries and emotional beats.

## Output checks

- Deliver the specified artifact, with decisions tied to identifiable scenes, beats or shots when relevant.
- Show evidence or reasoning for the selected approach and at least one plausible alternative when the decision is consequential; state whether it is planned or actually observed.
- Verify the specific failure mode above is not present, or document the creative reason and the test that would demonstrate success.
- Use the original practice case below to check that the procedure produces a concrete, reviewable result rather than a definition or generic advice.

## Practice case

A slow song includes a sustained phrase and a short instrumental response; select different shot holds and cuts based on phrasing, not multiples of five seconds.
