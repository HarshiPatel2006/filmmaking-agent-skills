# Conflict and stakes

## Inputs and deliverable

- Accept: premise; character goals; obstacles.
- Produce: conflict map, escalation ladder, irreversible choice.
- If critical inputs are missing, state assumptions, distinguish planning from observed footage, and mark any untestable decision for review. Do not invent measurements, assets or runtime results.

## Decision procedure

1. Name what each involved character wants in the present, and why their objectives cannot all be satisfied.
2. Choose opposition from inner, relational, social or situational forces as appropriate; make the opposing force capable of responding.
3. Express stakes as specific consequences of failure or inaction; separate immediate loss from deeper personal meaning.
4. Escalate by reducing options, increasing cost or exposing contradictions rather than merely adding danger.
5. Build a choice whose outcome changes a later scene; show consequences in behavior.

## Diagnose and correct

Do not confuse loud argument with dramatic conflict or assume every scene needs an antagonist. Quiet incompatible desires can be sufficient.

## Output checks

- Deliver the specified artifact, with decisions tied to identifiable scenes, beats or shots when relevant.
- Show evidence or reasoning for the selected approach and at least one plausible alternative when the decision is consequential; state whether it is planned or actually observed.
- Verify the specific failure mode above is not present, or document the creative reason and the test that would demonstrate success.
- Use the original practice case below to check that the procedure produces a concrete, reviewable result rather than a definition or generic advice.

## Practice case

A daughter wants to leave a family shop while her father needs her help; each escalation should narrow the choice without making either a caricature.
