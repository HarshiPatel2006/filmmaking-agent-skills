# Character motivation

## Inputs and deliverable

- Accept: premise; role; setting; relationship network.
- Produce: character profile with want, need, fears, choices and behavioral signature.
- If critical inputs are missing, state assumptions, distinguish planning from observed footage, and mark any untestable decision for review. Do not invent measurements, assets or runtime results.

## Decision procedure

1. Define an external objective and internal value or need; distinguish them where they conflict.
2. Specify physical presence, lived social circumstances and psychological tendencies insofar as they affect action.
3. Create a contradiction between self-image and behavior; give the character a tactic they use under pressure.
4. Define boundaries, relationships and an important choice; include what they will not do and what might change that.
5. Test three different situations and write observable actions, not personality labels; avoid treating astrology, body type or historical typologies as evidence.

## Diagnose and correct

A character defined only by appearance or diagnosis is underdeveloped. Replace labels with choices grounded in context.

## Output checks

- Deliver the specified artifact, with decisions tied to identifiable scenes, beats or shots when relevant.
- Show evidence or reasoning for the selected approach and at least one plausible alternative when the decision is consequential; state whether it is planned or actually observed.
- Verify the specific failure mode above is not present, or document the creative reason and the test that would demonstrate success.
- Use the original practice case below to check that the procedure produces a concrete, reviewable result rather than a definition or generic advice.

## Practice case

A talented but insecure architect is offered credit for someone else's work; specify desire, fear and behavior under two competing pressures.
