# Coverage planning

## Inputs and deliverable

- Accept: scene beats; staging; camera plan; resource constraints.
- Produce: shot list with priority, action ranges, camera setup and fallback.
- If critical inputs are missing, state assumptions, distinguish planning from observed footage, and mark any untestable decision for review. Do not invent measurements, assets or runtime results.

## Decision procedure

1. List each required beat and information item; assign primary coverage and identify missing reactions or transitions.
2. Choose master coverage for important spatial/action context; plan inserts, singles and cutaways that add meaning or solve a documented edit problem.
3. Record matched actions, dialogue timing, eyelines and sufficient edit handles; avoid relying on unrepeatable action without a fallback.
4. Group setups for efficiency while preserving continuity; prioritize essential shots over decorative alternatives.
5. Perform a paper edit and verify every beat can be assembled without gaps or contradictory action; mark optional coverage separately.

## Diagnose and correct

Do not shoot every angle just because it is available. Conversely, a single impressive shot may fail if its crucial beat is obscured.

## Output checks

- Deliver the specified artifact, with decisions tied to identifiable scenes, beats or shots when relevant.
- Show evidence or reasoning for the selected approach and at least one plausible alternative when the decision is consequential; state whether it is planned or actually observed.
- Verify the specific failure mode above is not present, or document the creative reason and the test that would demonstrate success.
- Use the original practice case below to check that the procedure produces a concrete, reviewable result rather than a definition or generic advice.

## Practice case

A scene has an interruption and two reactions; ensure the editor can change emphasis without inventing missing action.
