# Story architecture

## Inputs and deliverable

- Accept: premise; characters; runtime; format.
- Produce: causal outline, turning-point map, unresolved question ledger.
- If critical inputs are missing, state assumptions, distinguish planning from observed footage, and mark any untestable decision for review. Do not invent measurements, assets or runtime results.

## Decision procedure

1. Choose a structural model only if it fits the project: beginning-development-resolution for a compact arc, broader act or journey frameworks for a feature.
2. Define initial state, disruption, increasingly costly responses, decisive action and changed final state.
3. For each beat, state how the preceding choice causes or conditions it; use explicit bridges for nonchronological or associative storytelling.
4. Track viewer questions and answers separately from chronological events; align major revelations with emotional reversals.
5. Check setup/payoff, midpoint change and climax; scale length to form rather than fixed page percentages.

## Diagnose and correct

Do not force every film into a hero's journey or prescribed minute count. Use templates diagnostically; retain purposeful ambiguity.

## Output checks

- Deliver the specified artifact, with decisions tied to identifiable scenes, beats or shots when relevant.
- Show evidence or reasoning for the selected approach and at least one plausible alternative when the decision is consequential; state whether it is planned or actually observed.
- Verify the specific failure mode above is not present, or document the creative reason and the test that would demonstrate success.
- Use the original practice case below to check that the procedure produces a concrete, reviewable result rather than a definition or generic advice.

## Practice case

A three-minute film about an undelivered letter must contain consequential choices and a changed final image without padding to feature-film beats.
