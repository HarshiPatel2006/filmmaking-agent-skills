# Selective light and shadow

## Inputs and deliverable

- Accept: scene intent; blocking; target surfaces; tonal palette.
- Produce: selective illumination map, shadow purpose, readability test.
- If critical inputs are missing, state assumptions, distinguish planning from observed footage, and mark any untestable decision for review. Do not invent measurements, assets or runtime results.

## Decision procedure

1. Establish the narrative focal point and design dark and bright masses around it before adding detail.
2. Use controlled beams, flags, patterned shadows, negative fill or virtual equivalents to remove distracting illumination.
3. Separate planes with opposing tonal values; retain enough edge or texture for important action to read.
4. Use shadow deliberately to withhold information; plan when light reveals it, keeping source and geometry believable.
5. Check the image at final display brightness and in sequence; refine excessive black crush, clipping and visually distracting pools.

## Diagnose and correct

Avoid flooding every surface with light or hiding action required to understand the story. Darkness should be a designed relationship, not an exposure error.

## Output checks

- Deliver the specified artifact, with decisions tied to identifiable scenes, beats or shots when relevant.
- Show evidence or reasoning for the selected approach and at least one plausible alternative when the decision is consequential; state whether it is planned or actually observed.
- Verify the specific failure mode above is not present, or document the creative reason and the test that would demonstrate success.
- Use the original practice case below to check that the procedure produces a concrete, reviewable result rather than a definition or generic advice.

## Practice case

A character crosses a hallway and notices an open door; illuminate only the path and revealing detail without obscuring their reaction.
