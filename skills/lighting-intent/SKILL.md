# Lighting intent

## Inputs and deliverable

- Accept: scene goal; set plan; subjects; time; style.
- Produce: lighting brief with tonal key, focal priorities and change across beats.
- If critical inputs are missing, state assumptions, distinguish planning from observed footage, and mark any untestable decision for review. Do not invent measurements, assets or runtime results.

## Decision procedure

1. Choose what must be visible, what may remain obscure and how illumination should affect the relationship to the subject.
2. Identify plausible motivating sources and the dominant direction; decide whether realism or expressive abstraction serves the scene.
3. Plan the broad tonal organization of foreground, subjects and background before individual fixtures.
4. Establish subject readability at intended exposure; vary contrast and quality only with a narrative or environmental reason.
5. Check the wide, close and reverse coverage together; specify deviations as intentional rather than accidentally inconsistent.

## Diagnose and correct

Do not treat dark footage as inherently dramatic. Distinguish a designed dark composition with readable essential details from underexposure.

## Output checks

- Deliver the specified artifact, with decisions tied to identifiable scenes, beats or shots when relevant.
- Show evidence or reasoning for the selected approach and at least one plausible alternative when the decision is consequential; state whether it is planned or actually observed.
- Verify the specific failure mode above is not present, or document the creative reason and the test that would demonstrate success.
- Use the original practice case below to check that the procedure produces a concrete, reviewable result rather than a definition or generic advice.

## Practice case

A room shifts from welcoming to unsettling after a revelation; change attention and contrast, not merely brightness.
