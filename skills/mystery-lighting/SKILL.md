# Mystery lighting

## Inputs and deliverable

- Accept: scene beats; what viewer knows; location; source logic.
- Produce: reveal-by-light sequence, visibility targets, contrast rationale.
- If critical inputs are missing, state assumptions, distinguish planning from observed footage, and mark any untestable decision for review. Do not invent measurements, assets or runtime results.

## Decision procedure

1. List each piece of information the audience needs and each piece intentionally withheld.
2. Build darkness around readable islands of light; provide silhouettes or directional cues before identity reveal where appropriate.
3. Choose plausible sources such as a doorway, lamp or reflected light and decide which surfaces catch them.
4. Time light changes or movement into illumination with the dramatic beat; avoid indiscriminate flicker or random shadow patterns.
5. Review for spatial comprehension, essential expressions and the payoff of withheld information.

## Diagnose and correct

If darkness makes all action unintelligible, add a controlled cue or restructure the reveal. Not every suspense scene needs low-key light.

## Output checks

- Deliver the specified artifact, with decisions tied to identifiable scenes, beats or shots when relevant.
- Show evidence or reasoning for the selected approach and at least one plausible alternative when the decision is consequential; state whether it is planned or actually observed.
- Verify the specific failure mode above is not present, or document the creative reason and the test that would demonstrate success.
- Use the original practice case below to check that the procedure produces a concrete, reviewable result rather than a definition or generic advice.

## Practice case

A person enters a storage room and recognizes a familiar object; reveal object and reaction in a motivated pool while retaining unseen background.
