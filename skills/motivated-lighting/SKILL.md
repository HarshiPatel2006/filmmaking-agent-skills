# Motivated lighting

## Inputs and deliverable

- Accept: location; existing sources; blocking; desired contrast.
- Produce: source map with direction, quality, intensity relationships and subject zones.
- If critical inputs are missing, state assumptions, distinguish planning from observed footage, and mark any untestable decision for review. Do not invent measurements, assets or runtime results.

## Decision procedure

1. List sun, sky, windows, lamps and offscreen sources that plausibly illuminate the scene; distinguish visible practicals from supporting sources.
2. Choose a dominant direction and shape the subject with key; use fill or negative fill to set readable contrast rather than flattening form.
3. Use reflected or diffuse light to mimic environmental bounce; add separation or accents only if needed.
4. Account for shadows cast by subjects and furniture, practical exposure, movement through light and inverse-square effects where applicable.
5. Compare reverse angles and close-ups; preserve the apparent source even when adjusting actual placement for the shot.

## Diagnose and correct

Avoid multiple inconsistent hard shadows and unmotivated color casts. A practical may be visible without providing all required exposure.

## Output checks

- Deliver the specified artifact, with decisions tied to identifiable scenes, beats or shots when relevant.
- Show evidence or reasoning for the selected approach and at least one plausible alternative when the decision is consequential; state whether it is planned or actually observed.
- Verify the specific failure mode above is not present, or document the creative reason and the test that would demonstrate success.
- Use the original practice case below to check that the procedure produces a concrete, reviewable result rather than a definition or generic advice.

## Practice case

Two people sit next to a window and table lamp; choose which dominates and preserve the apparent direction on both faces.
