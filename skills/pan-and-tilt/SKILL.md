# Pan and tilt

## Inputs and deliverable

- Accept: start frame; target; action path; desired pacing.
- Produce: pan/tilt map with limits, start-end holds and edit cue.
- If critical inputs are missing, state assumptions, distinguish planning from observed footage, and mark any untestable decision for review. Do not invent measurements, assets or runtime results.

## Decision procedure

1. Decide whether the move follows a moving subject or reveals a new center of interest; establish the reason before motion.
2. Choose direction and arc using the space; preview the background and intervening elements for distractions.
3. Set start and end compositions first, including enough stationary footage to edit when appropriate.
4. Match angular speed to the subject or reveal; avoid unnecessary oscillation and sudden stops.
5. Check horizon and subject look/movement space throughout; consider a cut instead when traversed space adds no value.

## Diagnose and correct

A pan that sweeps irrelevant space delays comprehension. Shorten the path or cut between motivated views.

## Output checks

- Deliver the specified artifact, with decisions tied to identifiable scenes, beats or shots when relevant.
- Show evidence or reasoning for the selected approach and at least one plausible alternative when the decision is consequential; state whether it is planned or actually observed.
- Verify the specific failure mode above is not present, or document the creative reason and the test that would demonstrate success.
- Use the original practice case below to check that the procedure produces a concrete, reviewable result rather than a definition or generic advice.

## Practice case

Follow a person from a window to a nearby desk; specify where the pan starts and where it settles when the next action begins.
