# Premise and logline

## Inputs and deliverable

- Accept: creative seed; format; audience; constraints.
- Produce: premise, logline, audience promise, three distinct concept alternatives.
- If critical inputs are missing, state assumptions, distinguish planning from observed footage, and mark any untestable decision for review. Do not invent measurements, assets or runtime results.

## Decision procedure

1. Identify a specific person, their immediate desire and a problem that makes obtaining it costly.
2. Write three materially different premises, not three rewrites; vary the obstacle, choice or world.
3. Identify the causal engine: what action makes subsequent events necessary and what changes if the person succeeds or fails.
4. Select by dramatic potential, thematic resonance, filmability and distinctiveness; explain the tradeoff.
5. Condense to a sentence with protagonist, goal and obstacle; retain uncertainty rather than revealing every beat.

## Diagnose and correct

A mood or topic is not a premise. Replace abstract statements with an observable goal and consequential opposition; a poetic or documentary concept may use an organizing question instead of a fictional protagonist.

## Output checks

- Deliver the specified artifact, with decisions tied to identifiable scenes, beats or shots when relevant.
- Show evidence or reasoning for the selected approach and at least one plausible alternative when the decision is consequential; state whether it is planned or actually observed.
- Verify the specific failure mode above is not present, or document the creative reason and the test that would demonstrate success.
- Use the original practice case below to check that the procedure produces a concrete, reviewable result rather than a definition or generic advice.

## Practice case

A song about an impending farewell: distinguish a reunion with a deadline from a montage of beautiful strangers; the chosen concept should imply an ending and observable conflict.
