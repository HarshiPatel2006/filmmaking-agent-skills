# Production design brief

## Inputs and deliverable

- Accept: script; period; locations; character circumstances; format.
- Produce: design brief with visual rules, location/prop/wardrobe needs, feasibility decisions.
- If critical inputs are missing, state assumptions, distinguish planning from observed footage, and mark any untestable decision for review. Do not invent measurements, assets or runtime results.

## Decision procedure

1. Extract spaces, objects and physical conditions required by story actions and character circumstances.
2. Choose a visual vocabulary of materials, shapes, scale and selective colors; ground design in the story world, not generic luxury.
3. Define what must be visible from planned viewpoints, what may be implied, and how background details serve attention.
4. Coordinate layout, lighting opportunities and actor movement; identify design choices that would obstruct coverage.
5. Create reusable reference specifications and lock approved details before generating or shooting coverage; record intentional changes.

## Diagnose and correct

A beautiful set that prevents blocking or contradicts the character's means is poor production design. Revise around action and world logic.

## Output checks

- Deliver the specified artifact, with decisions tied to identifiable scenes, beats or shots when relevant.
- Show evidence or reasoning for the selected approach and at least one plausible alternative when the decision is consequential; state whether it is planned or actually observed.
- Verify the specific failure mode above is not present, or document the creative reason and the test that would demonstrate success.
- Use the original practice case below to check that the procedure produces a concrete, reviewable result rather than a definition or generic advice.

## Practice case

A cramped bakery is also a family meeting place; make the room support work, private talk and a revealing doorway.
