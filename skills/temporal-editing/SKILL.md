# Temporal editing

## Inputs and deliverable

- Accept: story chronology; desired reveal order; shots; viewer knowledge.
- Produce: screen-time order with temporal relationship, cues and rationale.
- If critical inputs are missing, state assumptions, distinguish planning from observed footage, and mark any untestable decision for review. Do not invent measurements, assets or runtime results.

## Decision procedure

1. Distinguish actual story chronology from presentation order and shot duration; label simultaneous, consecutive, earlier/later or ambiguous relationships.
2. Use continuity cutting for an uninterrupted action; omit routine intervals with ellipses when the viewer can infer them.
3. For flashbacks or leaps, establish temporal context using distinctive states, transitions or narrative cues instead of relying on a preset effect.
4. For parallel action, identify the relationship and intended expectation; alternate at meaningful beats rather than mechanically.
5. Review with a viewer who lacks the outline; ensure they infer the intended time relation or recognize deliberate ambiguity.

## Diagnose and correct

An unexplained costume change is not automatically a flashback. Provide an intelligible time cue or correct the mismatch.

## Output checks

- Deliver the specified artifact, with decisions tied to identifiable scenes, beats or shots when relevant.
- Show evidence or reasoning for the selected approach and at least one plausible alternative when the decision is consequential; state whether it is planned or actually observed.
- Verify the specific failure mode above is not present, or document the creative reason and the test that would demonstrate success.
- Use the original practice case below to check that the procedure produces a concrete, reviewable result rather than a definition or generic advice.

## Practice case

Intercut a letter being written yesterday and read today; create clear temporal cues without spelling out the chronology in dialogue.
