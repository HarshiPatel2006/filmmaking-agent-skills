# Composition hierarchy

## Inputs and deliverable

- Accept: shot purpose; subjects; set; frame format.
- Produce: composition specification with hierarchy, weight, lines and distraction check.
- If critical inputs are missing, state assumptions, distinguish planning from observed footage, and mark any untestable decision for review. Do not invent measurements, assets or runtime results.

## Decision procedure

1. Identify the first thing the viewer should notice and the second thing they should discover.
2. Use scale, placement, contrast, focus, motion, shape, direction and background separation to prioritize the subject.
3. Balance visual weights using actual image content; symmetry and thirds are options, not mandatory grids.
4. Inspect corners, borders, tangencies, unwanted reflections and competing highlights; shift framing or set dressing before adding effects.
5. Check composition at thumbnail and intended screen size; compare adjacent shots so attention transfers purposefully.

## Diagnose and correct

A bright background detail can dominate the intended face. Dim, mask, reframe or move it rather than indiscriminately centering the actor.

## Output checks

- Deliver the specified artifact, with decisions tied to identifiable scenes, beats or shots when relevant.
- Show evidence or reasoning for the selected approach and at least one plausible alternative when the decision is consequential; state whether it is planned or actually observed.
- Verify the specific failure mode above is not present, or document the creative reason and the test that would demonstrate success.
- Use the original practice case below to check that the procedure produces a concrete, reviewable result rather than a definition or generic advice.

## Practice case

A small figure in a bright hallway must be the first read; choose tonal separation and framing without losing spatial context.
