# Blocking and staging

## Inputs and deliverable

- Accept: scene; location plan; character tactics; constraints.
- Produce: blocking map with marks, pathways, axis, beat changes.
- If critical inputs are missing, state assumptions, distinguish planning from observed footage, and mark any untestable decision for review. Do not invent measurements, assets or runtime results.

## Decision procedure

1. Sketch a top view of the environment and mark entrances, obstacles, furniture and subject positions.
2. Assign each beat a dramatic reason for changes of position, distance, facing or physical level.
3. Check sightlines and occlusion from candidate cameras; design movement that reveals rather than accidentally masks important actions.
4. Choose whether subject movement or camera movement changes the staging; update line of action at new established positions.
5. Rehearse or visualize the full path and endpoints; record matching positions for coverage.

## Diagnose and correct

Do not move actors merely to keep the image active. If the movement obscures a crucial reaction or crosses an axis without cues, redesign it.

## Output checks

- Deliver the specified artifact, with decisions tied to identifiable scenes, beats or shots when relevant.
- Show evidence or reasoning for the selected approach and at least one plausible alternative when the decision is consequential; state whether it is planned or actually observed.
- Verify the specific failure mode above is not present, or document the creative reason and the test that would demonstrate success.
- Use the original practice case below to check that the procedure produces a concrete, reviewable result rather than a definition or generic advice.

## Practice case

Two people start apart at a kitchen island, one approaches after a confession; map the shift and the camera's view of the listener.
