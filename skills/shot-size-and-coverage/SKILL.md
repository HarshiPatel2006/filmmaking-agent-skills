# Shot size and coverage

## Inputs and deliverable

- Accept: scene; necessary information; adjacent shots; output aspect ratio.
- Produce: shot family with framing, purpose, coverage and transitions.
- If critical inputs are missing, state assumptions, distinguish planning from observed footage, and mark any untestable decision for review. Do not invent measurements, assets or runtime results.

## Decision procedure

1. Choose wide framing for spatial relationships when the audience needs orientation, medium framing for interaction, close framing for decisive detail or response.
2. Do not equate focal length with shot size; specify subject scale and camera position independently.
3. Plan coverage around unique narrative information rather than collecting arbitrary angles; include master, essential reactions and inserts only where useful.
4. Check scale and viewpoint differences between adjacent shots; preserve shared context or supply an intentional transition.
5. Record overlapping action or alternative entry and exit points for each important shot.

## Diagnose and correct

Avoid a progression of wide-medium-close as an automatic formula. Open in close-up when a later reveal is purposeful and orientation is not immediately needed.

## Output checks

- Deliver the specified artifact, with decisions tied to identifiable scenes, beats or shots when relevant.
- Show evidence or reasoning for the selected approach and at least one plausible alternative when the decision is consequential; state whether it is planned or actually observed.
- Verify the specific failure mode above is not present, or document the creative reason and the test that would demonstrate success.
- Use the original practice case below to check that the procedure produces a concrete, reviewable result rather than a definition or generic advice.

## Practice case

A key exchange occurs in a crowded room; use an establishing view, a relevant interaction view and a meaningful detail without redundant coverage.
