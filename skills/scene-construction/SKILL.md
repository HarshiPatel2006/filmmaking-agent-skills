# Scene construction

## Inputs and deliverable

- Accept: scene context; participants; narrative aim; format.
- Produce: scene outline or script with entry state, action beats, exit state.
- If critical inputs are missing, state assumptions, distinguish planning from observed footage, and mark any untestable decision for review. Do not invent measurements, assets or runtime results.

## Decision procedure

1. Define the scene's essential change in knowledge, relationship, decision or circumstance.
2. Give each active participant a local objective and tactic; translate feelings into observable behavior.
3. Enter near the first useful pressure or decision and omit routine preliminaries unless atmosphere or anticipation earns them.
4. Develop beats as shifts in tactics, information or control; ensure the ending changes the next scene.
5. Choose whether to show, imply or omit exposition; use dialogue only when it contributes something action cannot carry.
6. Read the scene in context and test whether removing it damages the story.

## Diagnose and correct

Do not label a scene dramatic simply because characters describe feelings. Replace explanation with a choice, gesture or consequence when possible.

## Output checks

- Deliver the specified artifact, with decisions tied to identifiable scenes, beats or shots when relevant.
- Show evidence or reasoning for the selected approach and at least one plausible alternative when the decision is consequential; state whether it is planned or actually observed.
- Verify the specific failure mode above is not present, or document the creative reason and the test that would demonstrate success.
- Use the original practice case below to check that the procedure produces a concrete, reviewable result rather than a definition or generic advice.

## Practice case

Two colleagues negotiate a resignation: the last action should alter their relationship or decision, not merely restate their positions.
