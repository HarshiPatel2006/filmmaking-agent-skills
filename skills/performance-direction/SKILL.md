# Performance direction

## Inputs and deliverable

- Accept: scene objective; character objectives; blocking; shot scale.
- Produce: performance beat sheet with action, stimulus, response and intensity.
- If critical inputs are missing, state assumptions, distinguish planning from observed footage, and mark any untestable decision for review. Do not invent measurements, assets or runtime results.

## Decision procedure

1. Replace abstract instructions such as be sad with an observable tactic, focus or physical action that a performer can vary.
2. Identify the stimulus for each reaction and the moment a character's tactic changes.
3. Tune movement and expression to framing and context; close framing can register small changes, wide framing may need clearer body behavior.
4. Give listeners actions and responses rather than making them wait passively for dialogue.
5. Maintain physical and emotional state across coverage; record a deliberate alternate performance for editing if needed.

## Diagnose and correct

Avoid imposing a fixed facial expression for the whole shot or exaggerated gestures for every beat. Direct objective and reaction, then judge the recorded performance.

## Output checks

- Deliver the specified artifact, with decisions tied to identifiable scenes, beats or shots when relevant.
- Show evidence or reasoning for the selected approach and at least one plausible alternative when the decision is consequential; state whether it is planned or actually observed.
- Verify the specific failure mode above is not present, or document the creative reason and the test that would demonstrate success.
- Use the original practice case below to check that the procedure produces a concrete, reviewable result rather than a definition or generic advice.

## Practice case

A character silently receives disappointing news in a medium close-up; specify what they try to conceal, when their attention shifts and how the hand action carries it.
