# Frame format

## Inputs and deliverable

- Accept: distribution formats; subjects; blocking; intended visual style.
- Produce: primary aspect ratio, composition boundaries, alternate crop plan.
- If critical inputs are missing, state assumptions, distinguish planning from observed footage, and mark any untestable decision for review. Do not invent measurements, assets or runtime results.

## Decision procedure

1. Select the primary frame shape based on relationship geometry and audience context, not a presumed cinematic ratio.
2. Design subject spacing, movement and environmental clues for that shape; use separate compositions when a secondary format cannot preserve them.
3. Mark essential content, look space and motion endpoints; distinguish safe regions from deliberate cropping.
4. Storyboard wide, medium and close views in the actual frame shape; compare alternate crops before production.
5. Review final exports for unwanted cuts of heads, hands, titles and narrative clues.

## Diagnose and correct

Do not simply center-crop a wide two-person scene into vertical if it removes the second character. Reframe or create alternate coverage.

## Output checks

- Deliver the specified artifact, with decisions tied to identifiable scenes, beats or shots when relevant.
- Show evidence or reasoning for the selected approach and at least one plausible alternative when the decision is consequential; state whether it is planned or actually observed.
- Verify the specific failure mode above is not present, or document the creative reason and the test that would demonstrate success.
- Use the original practice case below to check that the procedure produces a concrete, reviewable result rather than a definition or generic advice.

## Practice case

A wide dialogue and a vertical teaser share footage; identify shots needing alternative compositions.
