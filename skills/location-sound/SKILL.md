# Location sound

## Inputs and deliverable

- Accept: scene audio needs; sound source; location noise; equipment.
- Produce: sound capture plan with mic choice, monitoring, room tone and sync.
- If critical inputs are missing, state assumptions, distinguish planning from observed footage, and mark any untestable decision for review. Do not invent measurements, assets or runtime results.

## Decision procedure

1. Identify dialogue, ambient sound, music and effects that must be captured or added later; prioritize intelligibility and narrative need.
2. Select microphone position for close, clean sound without obstructing movement or framing; choose directional/lavalier options according to context.
3. Listen for room reflections, noise, wind, handling and interference; monitor with headphones and verify recording levels with a test.
4. Capture appropriate room tone or ambience and record sync references when separate picture/sound are used; log takes.
5. Do not change factual interview meaning through edits; verify sync, clipping and audibility in playback.

## Diagnose and correct

A distant onboard mic may capture more room than speaker. Move the mic nearer or change location; avoid pretending denoising will recover missing speech.

## Output checks

- Deliver the specified artifact, with decisions tied to identifiable scenes, beats or shots when relevant.
- Show evidence or reasoning for the selected approach and at least one plausible alternative when the decision is consequential; state whether it is planned or actually observed.
- Verify the specific failure mode above is not present, or document the creative reason and the test that would demonstrate success.
- Use the original practice case below to check that the procedure produces a concrete, reviewable result rather than a definition or generic advice.

## Practice case

A quiet interview beside a noisy window; compare closer mic and alternate location, record room tone and verify audio with a test.
