# Storyboard design

## Inputs and deliverable

- Accept: scene brief; beat map; frame format; location plan.
- Produce: ordered storyboard with framing, action, camera movement, audio and cut cues.
- If critical inputs are missing, state assumptions, distinguish planning from observed footage, and mark any untestable decision for review. Do not invent measurements, assets or runtime results.

## Decision procedure

1. Start with thumbnails of essential events and viewer information; sketch several alternative approaches before polishing.
2. Use a panel for each significant change in framing or action; indicate movement path, start/end framing and offscreen cues explicitly.
3. Keep camera positions and spatial continuity consistent with the blocking map; annotate intentional departures.
4. Include shot IDs, rough duration or timing range, action, dialogue/audio cue and adjacent-shot relationship.
5. Read panels as a sequence or timed animatic; revise shots that only look good individually, then identify required production assets.

## Diagnose and correct

Do not treat a collection of unconnected beautiful stills as a storyboard. Add sequence order, movement and timing evidence.

## Output checks

- Deliver the specified artifact, with decisions tied to identifiable scenes, beats or shots when relevant.
- Show evidence or reasoning for the selected approach and at least one plausible alternative when the decision is consequential; state whether it is planned or actually observed.
- Verify the specific failure mode above is not present, or document the creative reason and the test that would demonstrate success.
- Use the original practice case below to check that the procedure produces a concrete, reviewable result rather than a definition or generic advice.

## Practice case

Board a silent encounter involving a dropped object, a look and a recognition; ensure the viewer can reconstruct cause and effect.
