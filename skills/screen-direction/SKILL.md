# Screen direction

## Inputs and deliverable

- Accept: location plan; blocking; adjacent shots; viewer orientation.
- Produce: axis diagram and continuity decision per shot boundary.
- If critical inputs are missing, state assumptions, distinguish planning from observed footage, and mark any untestable decision for review. Do not invent measurements, assets or runtime results.

## Decision procedure

1. Draw the active line between interacting subjects or along movement; mark each camera on the relevant side and predicted screen direction.
2. Check complementary look directions and relative heights; a character looking screen-right should see a target placed accordingly in the established geometry.
3. If a new axis is needed, establish the new relationship through a shared/reestablishing shot, visible actor/camera movement or other unambiguous cues.
4. Check entrances/exits and moving subjects independently; preserve direction unless a change is shown or intentional.
5. Test the edited sequence visually; a geometric rule is a default serving comprehension, not a prohibition on expressive disorientation.

## Diagnose and correct

A reverse angle flips both characters to the same facing direction. Correct camera placement or add a motivated reorientation; do not rely on random cutaways.

## Output checks

- Deliver the specified artifact, with decisions tied to identifiable scenes, beats or shots when relevant.
- Show evidence or reasoning for the selected approach and at least one plausible alternative when the decision is consequential; state whether it is planned or actually observed.
- Verify the specific failure mode above is not present, or document the creative reason and the test that would demonstrate success.
- Use the original practice case below to check that the procedure produces a concrete, reviewable result rather than a definition or generic advice.

## Practice case

Three speakers rotate attention from one pair to another; establish the new gaze line before crossing the previous axis.
