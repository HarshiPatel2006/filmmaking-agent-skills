# Theme and motif

## Inputs and deliverable

- Accept: premise; theme candidate; ending; genre.
- Produce: theme statement, counterargument, motif evolution map.
- If critical inputs are missing, state assumptions, distinguish planning from observed footage, and mark any untestable decision for review. Do not invent measurements, assets or runtime results.

## Decision procedure

1. Phrase theme as a tension between values, not a message characters must recite.
2. Give opposing characters plausible, different responses to the tension; let choices and consequences test it.
3. Choose one or two concrete recurring images or objects; vary their context and meaning across the story.
4. Trace setup, transformation and payoff; remove decorative symbolism that duplicates dialogue.
5. Ask what a viewer could infer from the action without the stated theme.

## Diagnose and correct

Avoid universal color symbolism and rigid metaphor dictionaries. If a motif never changes meaning, cut or redesign it.

## Output checks

- Deliver the specified artifact, with decisions tied to identifiable scenes, beats or shots when relevant.
- Show evidence or reasoning for the selected approach and at least one plausible alternative when the decision is consequential; state whether it is planned or actually observed.
- Verify the specific failure mode above is not present, or document the creative reason and the test that would demonstrate success.
- Use the original practice case below to check that the procedure produces a concrete, reviewable result rather than a definition or generic advice.

## Practice case

A borrowed key appears at a first meeting and a departure; map how ownership changes its meaning rather than simply repeating the insert.
