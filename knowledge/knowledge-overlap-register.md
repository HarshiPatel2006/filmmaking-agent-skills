# Cross-source synthesis and conflict register

| Overlap | Canonical treatment | Why and exceptions |
|---|---|---|
| Shot scale (MC, KT, BA) | Shot size and coverage + close-ups and inserts | Mascelli offers explicit shot categories; Katz emphasizes relationships between neighboring frames; the appendix contributes practical coverage. Retain scale as a choice, not wide-medium-close formula. |
| Camera viewpoint (MC, KT) | Camera angle and perspective + point of view | Distinguish physical angle from viewer identification. High/low angles have contextual effects rather than fixed symbolic meanings. |
| Action axis (MC, KT) | Screen direction | Mascelli establishes direction conventions; Katz shows changing sightlines and reestablishment. Keep both continuity defaults and motivated exceptions. |
| Staging (KT, MC) | Two-person dialogue, group dialogue, mobile staging | Use Katz's detailed geometric staging as primary procedure, supported by Mascelli's relationship framing and reverse coverage. Use patterns as options, not prescribed blocking. |
| Close-ups (MC, AL, KT) | Close-ups and inserts + portrait lighting | Separate editorial purpose and camera grammar from face modeling; Alton's light placement is conditional on subject and world, not a universal beauty scheme. |
| Depth (AL, MC, KT) | Depth staging + subject separation | Combine visible surfaces and occlusion with tonal contrast and focus. Alton's preference for dark foreground and light background is one strategy, not a physical law. |
| Key and fill (AL, BA) | Motivated lighting + portrait lighting | Alton's selective control and functional light roles are richer than a simple three-light setup; the appendix adds practical exposure and white-balance checks. Reject fixed fixture counts and obsolete equipment. |
| Atmosphere (AL, KT) | Weather and atmosphere, mystery, night, practical effects | Use optical and narrative effects; do not reproduce historical dangerous rigging or equate atmosphere with uniformly dark exposure. |
| Time and editing (MC, KT, SW) | Temporal editing, cutting on action, editing rhythm | Combine Mascelli's continuity and cutting categories with Katz's question/answer sequencing and workbook's story causality. Screen time need not equal chronological time. |
| Screenwriting structure (SW) | Story architecture | The workbook includes multiple secondary-source story frameworks. Use them as diagnosis and alternatives, not a universal beat schedule; no claims of independently reading cited books. |
| Character typologies (SW) | Character motivation and backstory | Retain goal, social circumstances, behavior and choice. Do not use astrology, humors, body-shape typologies or stereotypes as validated psychology. |
| Rhythmic imagery (AL, KT, BA) | Theme and motif, editing rhythm, music-video workflow | Alton's musical analogy informs visual tonal contrast; Katz focuses shot flow; the appendix distinguishes narrative and performance videos. Audio phrase alignment and exact timestamps are modern applications, not extracted empirical rules. |
| Movement (KT, BA) | Camera movement intent, pan/tilt, tracking and choreography | Katz's dramatic options are primary; the appendix contributes operational restraint/stability. Do not ban motion or zoom when purposeful. |
| Production checks (AL, KT, BA) | Camera preflight, look development, coverage planning | Translate screen tests and preproduction visualization into modern capture/virtual tests; exclude obsolete format shopping advice. |

Selection criterion: contribution to the actual creative decision, explanatory specificity, relevance across mediums, ability to test the result, and compatibility with other principles. Preserve differences where the right answer depends on artistic intention rather than selecting one absolute rule.
