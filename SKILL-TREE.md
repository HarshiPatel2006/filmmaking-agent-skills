# Skill tree

62 independently callable skills. Choose a category, then read the relevant `SKILL.md`. Optional links are in the registry; load only what the task needs.

## 01 — Creative development (3)

- [Premise and logline](skills/premise-and-logline/SKILL.md) — Develop a film premise, hook and concise pitch from an idea, song, image or theme.
- [Theme and motif](skills/theme-and-motif/SKILL.md) — Translate an intended theme into recurring dramatic choices and visual motifs without preaching.
- [Genre and tone](skills/genre-and-tone/SKILL.md) — Select genre conventions and maintain a coherent emotional and visual tone while allowing intentional contrast.

## 02 — Storytelling (3)

- [Conflict and stakes](skills/conflict-and-stakes/SKILL.md) — Create dramatic opposition and escalate meaningful stakes through character decisions.
- [Story architecture](skills/story-architecture/SKILL.md) — Structure a feature, short or lyrical narrative using causal turning points and audience questions.
- [Visual information design](skills/visual-information-design/SKILL.md) — Plan cinematic reveals, anticipation, suspense and audience knowledge across shots and scenes.

## 03 — Screenwriting (4)

- [Scene construction](skills/scene-construction/SKILL.md) — Write or revise a dramatic scene with a clear purpose, playable action and meaningful change.
- [Dialogue and subtext](skills/dialogue-and-subtext/SKILL.md) — Write differentiated, playable dialogue and implied meanings while reducing expository speech.
- [Exposition and backstory](skills/exposition-and-backstory/SKILL.md) — Introduce world rules and character history economically, revealing information when it matters.
- [Script revision](skills/script-revision/SKILL.md) — Diagnose and revise a screenplay at structural, scene and line levels.

## 04 — Character development (4)

- [Character motivation](skills/character-motivation/SKILL.md) — Build multidimensional fictional characters with observable goals, values, contradictions and choices.
- [Character arc](skills/character-arc/SKILL.md) — Track emotional and behavioral change across story beats, including flat, negative or ambiguous arcs.
- [Character backstory](skills/character-backstory/SKILL.md) — Develop relevant fictional history and relationships that produce specific present behavior.
- [Performance direction](skills/performance-direction/SKILL.md) — Convert emotional intent into playable actions, reactions and restrained performance beats.

## 05 — Film directing (5)

- [Directorial interpretation](skills/directorial-interpretation/SKILL.md) — Translate a script into a unified visual and performance intention before choosing coverage.
- [Blocking and staging](skills/blocking-and-staging/SKILL.md) — Plan actor positions, movement, gaze and physical interaction to express dramatic relationships.
- [Two person dialogue](skills/two-person-dialogue/SKILL.md) — Stage and cover a two-person dialogue with purposeful master, paired views and relationship changes.
- [Group dialogue staging](skills/group-dialogue-staging/SKILL.md) — Stage three or more participants so attention, relationships and conversational axes remain legible.
- [Mobile staging](skills/mobile-staging/SKILL.md) — Design scenes where actors move through space while the camera or framing responds coherently.

## 06 — Cinematography (5)

- [Shot size and coverage](skills/shot-size-and-coverage/SKILL.md) — Choose shot sizes according to geography, action detail, relationship and editorial needs.
- [Camera angle and perspective](skills/camera-angle-and-perspective/SKILL.md) — Select camera height, position and perspective to control spatial clarity and audience relationship.
- [Point of view](skills/point-of-view/SKILL.md) — Control who the audience observes, identifies with, or sees through, including subjective and objective framing.
- [Close ups and inserts](skills/close-ups-and-inserts/SKILL.md) — Use close framing, detail inserts and reaction shots to reveal information or intensify emotional proximity.
- [Lens and focus](skills/lens-and-focus/SKILL.md) — Select field of view, camera distance, depth of field and focus behavior appropriate to a shot.

## 07 — Composition and visual grammar (4)

- [Composition hierarchy](skills/composition-hierarchy/SKILL.md) — Compose a frame around a clear center of interest and controlled visual weight.
- [Depth staging](skills/depth-staging/SKILL.md) — Create believable depth and layered composition using subject placement, perspective, light and occlusion.
- [Open closed framing](skills/open-closed-framing/SKILL.md) — Use open and closed framing, negative space and offscreen cues to control viewer involvement.
- [Frame format](skills/frame-format/SKILL.md) — Choose aspect ratio and framing safe areas for a coherent composition across intended delivery formats.

## 08 — Camera movement (4)

- [Camera movement intent](skills/camera-movement-intent/SKILL.md) — Choose static framing, subject movement or a camera move based on its narrative contribution.
- [Pan and tilt](skills/pan-and-tilt/SKILL.md) — Plan rotational camera moves that connect subjects, reveal space or follow action.
- [Tracking and dolly](skills/tracking-and-dolly/SKILL.md) — Design translating camera moves with perspective change, parallax, focus and dramatic motivation.
- [Tracking choreography](skills/tracking-choreography/SKILL.md) — Coordinate multiple centers of interest and actor movements within a traveling shot or long take.

## 09 — Lighting and shadow (6)

- [Lighting intent](skills/lighting-intent/SKILL.md) — Translate narrative mood and audience information into an intentional lighting plan.
- [Motivated lighting](skills/motivated-lighting/SKILL.md) — Design coherent key, fill, practical and bounce illumination from plausible sources.
- [Selective light and shadow](skills/selective-light-and-shadow/SKILL.md) — Shape light and darkness to guide attention, imply offscreen space and convey dramatic meaning.
- [Portrait lighting](skills/portrait-lighting/SKILL.md) — Light a face or close-up to preserve identity, modeling, eye readability and intended character expression.
- [Subject separation](skills/subject-separation/SKILL.md) — Use tonal contrast, edges, backgrounds, focus and layers to separate subjects without overlighting.
- [Lighting continuity](skills/lighting-continuity/SKILL.md) — Maintain a believable lighting world across master shots, close-ups, camera moves and different takes.

## 10 — Atmosphere and special illumination (5)

- [Mystery lighting](skills/mystery-lighting/SKILL.md) — Design suspense and mystery through selective visibility, silhouettes and gradual revelation.
- [Night and street lighting](skills/night-and-street-lighting/SKILL.md) — Create readable night interiors or exteriors with believable local pools, ambient sky and practical sources.
- [Weather and atmosphere](skills/weather-and-atmosphere/SKILL.md) — Design rain, fog, snow and environmental depth that remain visually legible and physically coherent.
- [Fire and practical effects](skills/fire-and-practical-effects/SKILL.md) — Design believable candle, fire, lightning and other changing-source illumination without unsafe historical rigging.
- [Exterior lighting](skills/exterior-lighting/SKILL.md) — Plan outdoor cinematography around sun, sky, bounce, weather, subject position and time-of-day continuity.

## 11 — Production design and visual world (4)

- [Production design brief](skills/production-design-brief/SKILL.md) — Translate narrative requirements into a coherent visual world, set design and art-direction brief.
- [Location geography](skills/location-geography/SKILL.md) — Plan consistent locations, camera access, environmental landmarks and spatial continuity.
- [Prop and costume continuity](skills/prop-and-costume-continuity/SKILL.md) — Design and track props, wardrobe and appearance changes that support narrative action and continuity.
- [Look development](skills/look-development/SKILL.md) — Develop a coherent photographic and design style using comparative tests rather than vague style labels.

## 12 — Previsualization and coverage (4)

- [Storyboard design](skills/storyboard-design/SKILL.md) — Translate narrative beats into a sequence of original frame sketches or textual panels that communicate shot flow.
- [Coverage planning](skills/coverage-planning/SKILL.md) — Build a shot list that supplies the editor with necessary narrative coverage and feasible alternatives.
- [Camera angle projection](skills/camera-angle-projection/SKILL.md) — Use simplified top and elevation views to estimate camera placement, framing and feasibility.
- [Animatic and shot flow](skills/animatic-and-shot-flow/SKILL.md) — Build and evaluate a timed previsualization before expensive filming or generation.

## 13 — Continuity (2)

- [Screen direction](skills/screen-direction/SKILL.md) — Preserve or deliberately change screen direction, action axes and eyelines across coverage.
- [Action continuity](skills/action-continuity/SKILL.md) — Maintain physical and temporal action states across takes, angles, inserts and edits.

## 14 — Editing and film rhythm (4)

- [Temporal editing](skills/temporal-editing/SKILL.md) — Construct coherent film time through continuity, ellipsis, flashbacks, parallel action and deliberate temporal changes.
- [Cutting on action](skills/cutting-on-action/SKILL.md) — Choose and refine cuts that continue movement, preserve direction and transfer attention smoothly.
- [Editing rhythm](skills/editing-rhythm/SKILL.md) — Choose shot duration and cut placement from narrative information, performance, movement and soundtrack cues.
- [Transitions and montage](skills/transitions-and-montage/SKILL.md) — Use straight cuts, dissolves, match transitions, associative montage and parallel structures with purpose.

- [Location sound](skills/location-sound/SKILL.md) — Plan intelligible production sound, microphone placement, monitoring and usable ambience.
- [Event and interview coverage](skills/event-and-interview-coverage/SKILL.md) — Plan coverage for documentary interviews, live events and performances with limited repeatability.
